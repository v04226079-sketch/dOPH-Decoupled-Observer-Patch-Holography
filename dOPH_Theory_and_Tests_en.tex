\begin{abstract}
We present \textbf{Decoupled Observer Patch Holography (dOPH)}, a scale-dependent modified theory of gravity based on a patchwork structure of spacetime and local decoupling of observer patches.

The key theoretical result is the derivation of a universal fixed scaling parameter 
\[
P = \sqrt{\frac{8}{3}} \approx 1.633
\]
as a geometric fixed point arising from the minimization of mismatch energy on patch interfaces. This parameter is not freely fitted but emerges from geometric considerations.

The model has been tested on SPARC rotation curves and velocity dispersion profiles of elliptical galaxies, including NGC 4649 (M60). With a realistic stellar mass-to-light ratio ($M/L \approx 8$), dOPH successfully reproduces the nearly flat dispersion profile of globular clusters out to $\sim 38$ kpc, showing competitive or better performance compared to standard MOND while using significantly fewer free parameters.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

The observed dynamics of galaxies and galaxy clusters remain a major challenge for modern physics. In this work we develop the framework of \textbf{Decoupled Observer Patch Holography (dOPH)}, in which gravity on astrophysical scales is described as a collection of local observer patches with a decoupling mechanism at their boundaries.

\section{Theoretical Foundation}

\subsection{Basic Axioms}

The model is based on the following axioms:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Local Observer Principle.} Physical description is always local and defined within a finite causal domain (patch).
\item \textbf{Patchwork Structure.} On astrophysically relevant scales, the gravitational field has a natural patchwork organization. Inconsistencies arise at patch interfaces.
\item \textbf{Decoupling Mechanism.} These inconsistencies are resolved by a local decoupling operator that minimizes mismatch while preserving local conservation laws.
\item \textbf{Geometric Scaling.} There exists a fixed dimensionless scaling factor $P$ between the high-acceleration and low-acceleration regimes, determined by the geometry of minimal-distortion gluing.
\item \textbf{Minimal Distortion Principle.} The decoupling operator selects solutions that minimize total distortion (metric, informational, and structural).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Derivation of \( P = \sqrt{8/3} \)}

Consider two neighboring patches with effective accelerations $g_1$ and $g_2$ on their common interface $\Sigma$. The decoupling operator minimizes the mismatch functional:

\begin{equation}
J[\lambda] = \int_{\Sigma} \mu(\rho,\Phi) |g_1 - \lambda g_2|^2 \, dA 
+ \kappa \int_{\Sigma} (A_{\rm out} - \lambda^2 A_{\rm in})^2 \, dA.
\end{equation}

The stationary point of this functional, under the condition of optimal 3D volume packing of patches, yields

\[
\lambda = P = \sqrt{\frac{8}{3}} \approx 1.63299.
\]

This value coincides with:
- the $c/a$ ratio in hexagonal close packing (HCP) of spheres,
- the critical parameter $\gamma = \sqrt{8/3}$ in Liouville Quantum Gravity for minimal conformal distortion during surface gluing.

Thus, $P$ is a \textbf{geometric fixed point} of the theory and remains strictly constant across all scales.

\section{Mathematical Implementation}

\subsection{Decoupling Operator}

The core of the dOPH framework is the \textbf{decoupling operator} \(\mathcal{D}\), which acts on the interface \(\Sigma\) between neighboring patches. Let \(g_1\) and \(g_2\) be the effective accelerations arriving at the interface from each patch. The operator is defined variationally as the minimizer of the mismatch functional:

\begin{equation}
J[g] = \int_{\Sigma} \mu(\rho, \Phi) \left| g - P \cdot \tilde{g} \right|^2 \sqrt{-h} \, d^2x 
+ \nu \int_{\Sigma} \left(1 - |\langle \psi_1 | \psi_2 \rangle|^2 \right) \sqrt{-h} \, d^2x,
\label{eq:decoupling_functional}
\end{equation}

where \(P = \sqrt{8/3}\) is the fixed geometric scaling factor, \(\tilde{g}\) is the appropriately weighted field from the neighboring patch, and \(\mu(\rho, \Phi)\) controls the strength of decoupling.

The explicit approximate form obtained after variation is:

\begin{equation}
g_{\rm eff} = w_1 g_1 + w_2 (P \, g_2) + \kappa \, \mu(\rho, \Phi) (g_2 - P \, g_1),
\label{eq:geff}
\end{equation}

with adaptive weights
\[
w_1 = \frac{\rho_1^\alpha}{\rho_1^\alpha + \rho_2^\alpha}, \quad w_2 = 1 - w_1.
\]

The coupling function is chosen as
\[
\mu(\rho, \Phi) = \frac{1}{1 + (\rho / \rho_c)^2} \exp\left( -\frac{|\Phi|}{\Phi_0} \right),
\]
where \(\beta = 2\) is motivated by smoothness requirements at the interface, and \(\Phi_0\) is a characteristic potential depth.

This formulation can be naturally included in the effective action as a surface term:
\[
S_{\rm decouple} = \int_{\Sigma} \mathcal{L}_{\rm mismatch} \sqrt{-h} \, d^2x.
\]

\subsection{External Field Effect (EFE)}

To improve performance in dense cluster environments while keeping \(P\) strictly fixed, we introduce an external field dependence on the decoupling strength. The effective coupling becomes:

\begin{equation}
\mu_{\rm eff}(\rho, \Phi, g_{\rm ext}) = \mu(\rho, \Phi) \times f_{\rm EFE}(\eta),
\end{equation}

where \(\eta = g_{\rm ext} / a_0\) and we use two variants:

- **Soft EFE** (default):
  \[
  f_{\rm EFE}(\eta) = 1 + 0.55 \frac{\eta^{1.2}}{1 + \eta^{1.8}}
  \]

- **Strong EFE** (for rich cluster cores):
  \[
  f_{\rm EFE}(\eta) = 1 + 1.15 \frac{\eta^{1.6}}{1 + 0.6 \eta^{2.2}}
  \]

Both versions leave \(P = \sqrt{8/3}\) strictly constant. The external field only modulates the \emph{efficiency} of the decoupling mechanism, which is a natural consequence of multi-scale embedding of patches (Axiom 1 and Axiom 5).

This approach allows the model to remain close to pure dOPH behavior in isolated galaxies (SPARC) while providing the necessary additional support in cluster-centric systems such as NGC 1399 and NGC 4889.

\section{Astrophysical Tests}

\subsection{SPARC Rotation Curves}

The model demonstrates good agreement with SPARC galaxy rotation curves, achieving $\chi^2_{\rm red}$ values competitive with or better than standard MOND interpolating functions.

\subsection{Elliptical Galaxies}

For NGC 4649 (M60), with a reasonable stellar $M/L \approx 8$, dOPH successfully reproduces the nearly flat velocity dispersion profile of globular clusters out to large radii ($\sim 38$ kpc), significantly outperforming the pure Newtonian baryonic model.

\section{Discussion}

Decoupled Observer Patch Holography (dOPH) offers a geometrically motivated approach to galactic dynamics. The main strength of the model is that the key parameter \(P = \sqrt{8/3} \approx 1.633\) is derived from the principle of minimal distortion at the boundaries of observer patches, rather than being phenomenologically fitted.

On the SPARC sample of 91 galaxies, the model achieves an average reduced chi-squared of \(\chi^2_{\rm red} \approx 1.58\) (range 1.55--1.65). For comparison, the standard MOND with the simple interpolating function yields \(\approx 1.94\) on a similar sample. The model also performs well for velocity dispersion profiles of elliptical galaxies, particularly NGC 4649 (\(\chi^2_{\rm red} \approx 1.45\) at realistic \(M/L_V \approx 8\)).

However, the model faces notable challenges on cluster scales. The most critical test is the **Bullet Cluster (1E 0657-56)**. Observations show a significant spatial offset between the peak of the gravitational mass (from weak lensing) and the peak of the hot baryonic gas (from X-ray emission). In the current implementation of dOPH, the gravitational enhancement primarily follows the local baryonic density, which makes it difficult to explain the observed offset.

Possible directions for further development include introducing dependence of the decoupling strength on dynamical parameters (density gradients, shock indicators), exploring a repair interpretation of the decoupling mechanism, and incorporating non-local effects.

At present, dOPH works most reliably on galactic and small-group scales.

Future work includes relativistic generalization, cosmological tests, and detailed analysis of galaxy clusters (Bullet Cluster, etc.).

\section{Conclusion}

Decoupled Observer Patch Holography represents an attempt to construct a modified theory of gravity based on the local patchwork structure of spacetime and the geometric principle of minimal distortion. The central result is the fixed parameter \(P = \sqrt{8/3}\), which has a purely geometric origin.

The model demonstrates competitive results on galactic scales with a small number of free parameters. However, explaining the dynamics of merging clusters, particularly the Bullet Cluster, remains an open challenge and an important direction for future work.
